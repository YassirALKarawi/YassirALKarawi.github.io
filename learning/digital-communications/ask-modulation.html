The central idea

In binary ASK, the amplitude of a changes according to the transmitted bit. A common form, called , sends the carrier for bit 1 and suppresses it for bit 0.

Engineering meaning

ASK is simple to generate, but amplitude disturbances and noise can make its receiver less robust than phase-based modulation.

02

Mathematical signal model

Let each bit occupy the interval 0 ≤ t < Tb. Binary ASK uses two possible waveforms:

s1(t) = A cos(2πfct + φ),   0 ≤ t < Tb
s0(t) = 0,   0 ≤ t < Tb
s(t) = A m(t) cos(2πfct + φ)
A
Peak carrier amplitude, V
m(t)
Unipolar binary message, 0 or 1
fc

φ
Initial carrier phase, rad
Tb
Bit duration, s
Rb
Bit rate = 1/Tb, bit/s

Signal-space representation

Choose the unit-energy basis function φ1(t) = √(2/Tb) cos(2πfct). The two signals become points on one axis:

s1 = √E1,    s0 = 0,    d = √E1

The Euclidean distance d controls how easily a receiver separates the two hypotheses in additive white Gaussian noise.

A sufficiently high carrier frequency gives multiple carrier cycles within each bit interval. This supports practical bandpass transmission, filtering and coherent demodulation. The exact ratio is a design choice rather than a universal constant.

03

Energy, power and bandwidth

Energy of the two symbols

For an integer number of carrier cycles within Tb, the integral of cos² over the bit interval is Tb/2:

E1 = ∫0Tb s1²(t)dt= A² ∫0Tb cos²(2πfct)dt= A²Tb/2E0 = 0

Average energy and power

If bits 0 and 1 are equally probable, P(1)=P(0)=1/2:

Eb,avg = P(1)E1 + P(0)E0 = E1/2
Pavg = Eb,avg/Tb = A²/4

For a general probability p=P(1), Eb,avg=pE1 and Pavg=pA²/2.

Bandwidth

For rectangular non-return-to-zero data pulses, the baseband spectrum has its first null at Rb. Translation around ±fc gives an approximate null-to-null passband bandwidth:

BNN ≈ 2Rb

Practical bandwidth depends on pulse shaping and the definition used, such as null-to-null, 3 dB, or occupied bandwidth. Raised-cosine shaping gives B ≈ (1+α)Rb, where α is the roll-off factor.

05

Transmitter and coherent receiver

Transmitter

Binary data m(t)×Carrier oscillator→ASK signal s(t)

A product modulator or an electronic switch multiplies the carrier by the unipolar message. When m(t)=1 the carrier passes; when m(t)=0 it is suppressed.

Coherent receiver

The receiver needs a local carrier synchronized in frequency and phase. A product detector correlates the received waveform with that reference. The low-pass filter or integrator removes the doubled-frequency term, and the decision device compares the sampled statistic with a threshold.

Choose bit 1 if z > γChoose bit 0 if z ≤ γ

For equal prior probabilities, equal error costs and the normalized OOK model, the optimum threshold lies midway between the two noiseless correlator outputs.

Detection and bit-error probability

In an additive white Gaussian noise channel, r(t)=si(t)+n(t). Coherent correlation reduces the waveform decision to a scalar observation z. For equiprobable OOK symbols:

Pb = Q(√(E1/(2N0))) = Q(√(Eb,avg/N0))

Here Q(x)=(1/√(2π))∫x∞e−u²/2du. This expression assumes coherent detection, equally likely bits and Eb,avg=E1/2. Changing the definition of Eb changes the appearance of the formula, so the energy convention must always be stated.

Coherent versus noncoherent ASK

Coherent detection generally offers better noise performance but requires carrier phase synchronization. Envelope detection is simpler and does not require phase recovery, although its error performance differs.

Worked examples

Example 1 · Energy of a transmitted 1

Given: A=4 V, a normalized 1 Ω load and Tb=1 ms. Find E1, assuming an integer number of carrier cycles.

  1. E1 = ∫0Tb s²(t) dt
  2. E1 = A² ∫0Tb cos²(2πfct) dt
  3. E1 = A²Tb/2
  4. E1 = (4² × 0.001)/2 = 0.008 J = 8 mJ

8.000 mJ

Example 2 · Bit rate and bandwidth

Given: Tb=20 μs and rectangular OOK pulses.

Rb=1/Tb=1/(20×10−6)=50 kbit/s.

BNN≈2Rb=100 kHz.

Example 3 · Average power for unequal bits

Given: A=2 V and P(1)=0.3 across a normalized 1 Ω load.

Pavg=pA²/2=0.3×2²/2=0.6 W.

The source transmits less average power than an equiprobable sequence because the carrier is present for only 30% of the bits.

Practice problems

Attempt each problem before opening the answer. The numerical questions include units deliberately; convert them before substitution.

1. A=5 V and Tb=2 ms. Find E1.

E1=A²Tb/2=25×0.002/2=25 mJ.

2. Rb=200 kbit/s. Estimate the rectangular OOK null-to-null bandwidth.

BNN≈2Rb=400 kHz.

3. A=3 V and P(1)=0.6. Find average transmitted power for a normalized 1 Ω load.

Pavg=pA²/2=0.6×9/2=2.7 W.

4. Explain why a threshold that is too high increases one type of error.

A high threshold makes a transmitted 1 more likely to fall below γ, increasing 1→0 errors.

5. Why does coherent detection require a carrier-recovery circuit?

The local oscillator must match the received carrier frequency and phase so correlation adds the desired signal coherently rather than attenuating it.

6. If A doubles, how do E1 and average power change?

Both are proportional to A², so each becomes four times its original value.

7. Compare ASK with BPSK qualitatively.

ASK encodes information in amplitude and is sensitive to amplitude disturbances. BPSK uses phase reversal and, for comparable coherent binary signalling conventions, generally provides better power efficiency.

8. Design task: choose Rb for BNN≤1 MHz using rectangular OOK.

2Rb≤1 MHz, therefore Rb≤500 kbit/s.

10

Notation, summary and references

Essential results

  • OOK is binary ASK with s1(t)=A cos(2πfct) and s0(t)=0.
  • E1=A²Tb/2 for an integer number of carrier cycles.
  • For equiprobable bits, Eb,avg=E1/2 and Pavg=A²/4.
  • Rectangular OOK has approximate null-to-null bandwidth BNN≈2Rb.
  • A coherent receiver uses carrier synchronization, correlation or product detection, filtering, sampling and threshold decision.

01

Start with meaning

Why this topic matters

Band-limited channels spread pulses in time, so neighbouring symbols can interfere at the decision instant. Nyquist pulse shaping controls this overlap: pulses may overlap everywhere except at the exact sampling instants.

INTUITION

A clean eye diagram is an open timing window. Noise closes it vertically; timing error and dispersion close it horizontally. The receiver should sample near the widest opening.

02

Mathematical foundation

Understand, derive, then calculate

Every expression begins with its physical purpose and ends with an engineering interpretation. Read the explanation first; the equation is a compressed statement of that explanation.

01

Zero-ISI samples

Before the equation Use this relation when the problem asks about zero-isi samples. First list the known quantities, convert them to SI units, and verify the stated assumptions.

p(nT)=\begin{cases}1,&n=0\\0,&n\ne0\end{cases}
Engineering interpretation

Other pulses contribute zero at the target symbol instant.

02

Raised-cosine bandwidth

Before the equation Use this relation when the problem asks about raised-cosine bandwidth. First list the known quantities, convert them to SI units, and verify the stated assumptions.

B=\frac{1+\alpha}{2T}=\frac{(1+\alpha)R_s}{2}
Engineering interpretation

One-sided baseband bandwidth.

03

Overall response

Before the equation Use this relation when the problem asks about overall response. First list the known quantities, convert them to SI units, and verify the stated assumptions.

H_{RC}(f)=H_{RRC,Tx}(f)H_{RRC,Rx}(f)
Engineering interpretation

Two root-raised-cosine filters form a raised-cosine response.

04

Received samples

Before the equation Use this relation when the problem asks about received samples. First list the known quantities, convert them to SI units, and verify the stated assumptions.

y(kT)=a_k+\sum_{n\ne k}a_n p((k-n)T)+w_k
Engineering interpretation

The sum is ISI; wₖ is noise.

STEP-BY-STEP DERIVATION

Raised-cosine bandwidth

The Nyquist minimum one-sided bandwidth is Rₛ/2. Roll-off α adds a controlled transition band.

  1. Start with the minimum edge

    B_0=\frac{R_s}{2}

  2. Add the roll-off fraction

    B=B_0+\alpha B_0

  3. Factor the result

    B=\frac{(1+\alpha)R_s}{2}

  4. Check the limits

    \alpha=0\Rightarrow B=R_s/2,\quad\alpha=1\Rightarrow B=R_s
How to study the mathematics

First name every symbol, then state the assumptions, substitute values with units, and only then calculate. A numerically correct result without assumptions is not a complete engineering answer.

01Where should the sampler operate in an eye?
Guided solution

Near the centre of the widest opening.

02What does vertical eye closure indicate?
Guided solution

Reduced amplitude/noise margin.

03Why split RC filtering into two RRC filters?
Guided solution

To share implementation between transmitter and receiver while keeping an RC cascade.

Guided solution

Other pulses contribute zero at the target symbol instant.

05Draw the complete processing order from memory.
Guided solution

Symbols → RRC pulse shaper → Band-limited channel → Noise → Matched RRC → Timing sampler → Decisions.

06Increase the laboratory impairment from 0.15 to 0.80. What should change?
Guided solution

The received observation should move farther from its ideal reference, reduce the visual decision margin, or show stronger distortion; the precise appearance depends on this lesson's model.

07Compare “Zero-ISI samples” with “Raised-cosine bandwidth”.
Guided solution

The first relation establishes other pulses contribute zero at the target symbol instant. The second establishes one-sided baseband bandwidth.

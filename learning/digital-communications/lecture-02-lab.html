Analog pulse modulation

Compare natural and flat-top PAM, pulse width and pulse position. Measure each edge and recover the message sample.

One sample, three representations

MethodMessage changesReceiver measures
PAMPulse heightAmplitude
PWMPulse widthTime between the two edges
PPMPulse positionDelay from a synchronized reference

Natural PAM follows the message while its gate is on. Flat-top PAM captures one sample and holds that value. PWM and PPM keep pulse height fixed.

τk = τ0 + kwx(kTs)

In this laboratory, Ts = 100 μs, τ0 = 50 μs and kw = 20 μs/V. PPM starts at the PWM falling edge and lasts 10 μs. The receiver subtracts the baseline delay and divides by sensitivity.

x(kTs) = (dk − τ0)/kw

PAM spectral copies must be separated for LPF recovery. Natural-PAM gain correction is 1/d, where d = τ/Ts. Flat-top holding also introduces a sinc-shaped aperture response.

Worked practice

Find PWM width for x = 1.5 V.

Law: τ = 50 + 20x μs.

Substitute: τ = 50 + 20(1.5).

Result: τ = 80 μs.

Recover a PPM sample with delay 70 μs.

Law: x = (d − 50)/20 V.

Remove offset: 70 − 50 = 20 μs.

Result: x = 20/20 = 1 V.

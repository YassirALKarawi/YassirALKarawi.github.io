01

Start with meaning

Why this topic matters

Sampling converts a continuous-time waveform into a sequence while preserving its information when the rate is high enough. In frequency, sampling creates shifted copies of the original spectrum. Reconstruction succeeds only when these copies do not overlap.

INTUITION

A camera that photographs a rotating wheel too slowly can make it appear to reverse. Aliasing in communication signals is the same ambiguity: several continuous frequencies produce the same samples.

02

Mathematical foundation

Equations you can trust

Read every formula as a relationship between physical quantities. Check units before inserting numbers, and keep linear ratios separate from decibels.

01

Sampling theorem

f_s>2B

The strict inequality provides practical transition margin.

02

Sampling sequence

x[n]=x(nT_s),\quad T_s=1/f_s

Continuous time is observed at integer multiples of Tₛ.

03

Aliased frequency

f_a=|f-kf_s|

Choose integer k so that 0≤fₐ≤fₛ/2.

04

Ideal reconstruction

x(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{t-nT_s}{T_s}\right)

Every sample weights a shifted sinc pulse.

How to study the mathematics

First name every symbol, then state the assumptions, substitute values with units, and only then calculate. A numerically correct result without assumptions is not a complete engineering answer.

From symbols to numbers

Fully worked examples

Worked example 1

Minimum sampling rate

Problem

A voice signal is band-limited to 3.4 kHz. Determine the theoretical minimum fₛ.

  1. B=3.4 kHz
  2. fₛ>2B=6.8 kHz
AnswerA practical system may choose 8 kHz.
Worked example 2

Aliasing

Problem

A 7 kHz sinusoid is sampled at 10 kHz. What frequency appears?

  1. Nyquist interval ends at 5 kHz
  2. fₐ=|7−10|=3 kHz
AnswerThe samples are indistinguishable from a 3 kHz sinusoid.
06

Deliberate practice

Problems with guided solutions

Attempt each problem on paper before opening the solution. Include units and state the formula you selected.

01Find the Nyquist rate for B=20 kHz.
Solution

40 ksample/s; practical designs use a larger value.

02A 9 kHz tone sampled at 12 kHz aliases to what frequency?
Solution

|9−12|=3 kHz.

03Why place a low-pass filter before the sampler?
Solution

To remove content above fₛ/2 before it aliases irreversibly.

04Find Tₛ for fₛ=48 kHz.
Solution

20.833 μs.

05What does the reconstruction LPF remove?
Solution

The spectral images centred at integer multiples of fₛ.

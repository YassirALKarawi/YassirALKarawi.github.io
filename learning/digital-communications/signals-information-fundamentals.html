01

Start with meaning

Why this topic matters

A digital link carries information by selecting one waveform from a finite alphabet during every symbol interval. This first lesson separates the ideas students most often mix up: bit rate, symbol rate, bandwidth, energy per bit and power. Once these quantities are connected dimensionally, later modulation formulas become much easier to trust.

INTUITION

Think of a symbol as one physical gesture and bits as the meaning packed into that gesture. A binary gesture carries one bit; a 16-ary gesture carries four bits. The channel sees waveforms, not the labels 0 and 1.

02

Mathematical foundation

Equations you can trust

Read every formula as a relationship between physical quantities. Check units before inserting numbers, and keep linear ratios separate from decibels.

01

Bits per symbol

k=\log_2 M

An M-ary alphabet represents k bits per symbol.

02

Symbol rate

R_s=\frac{R_b}{\log_2 M}

Baud equals symbols per second, not necessarily bits per second.

03

Energy per bit

E_b=\frac{P}{R_b}=PT_b

Average power multiplied by bit duration.

04

Spectral efficiency

\eta=\frac{R_b}{B}\quad[\mathrm{bit/s/Hz}]

Useful information rate per hertz.

How to study the mathematics

First name every symbol, then state the assumptions, substitute values with units, and only then calculate. A numerically correct result without assumptions is not a complete engineering answer.

From symbols to numbers

Fully worked examples

Worked example 1

16-QAM rate conversion

Problem

A 16-QAM modem carries 24 Mbit/s. Find k and the symbol rate.

  1. k=log₂(16)=4 bit/symbol
  2. Rₛ=24/4=6 Msymbol/s
AnswerThe modem operates at 6 Mbaud.
Worked example 2

Energy from power

Problem

A transmitter delivers 20 mW at 2 Mbit/s. Find Eᵦ.

  1. Convert power: 20 mW=0.020 W
  2. Eᵦ=P/Rᵦ=0.020/(2×10⁶)
AnswerEᵦ=10 nJ.

Deliberate practice

Problems with guided solutions

Attempt each problem on paper before opening the solution. Include units and state the formula you selected.

01A QPSK link sends 12 Mbit/s. Find Rₛ.
Solution

QPSK has k=2, so Rₛ=6 Mbaud.

02Find Tᵦ at 5 Mbit/s.
Solution

Tᵦ=1/Rᵦ=0.2 μs.

03A link sends 10 Mbit/s in 4 MHz. Find η.
Solution

η=2.5 bit/s/Hz.

04How many bits does one 64-QAM symbol carry?
Solution

log₂64=6 bits.

05Why is baud not always bit/s?
Solution

One M-ary symbol can encode more than one bit.

From a message to a decision

A source produces information. The transmitter prepares a signal, the channel carries it, and the receiver estimates the message. Noise and distortion can change the received waveform.

Source → Transmitter → Channel → Receiver → Destination

Three distinct operations

Sampling selects measurement times. Quantization selects amplitude levels. Encoding assigns a binary label to each level.

Ts = 1/fs   ·   fN = 2B

B is the highest baseband message frequency in hertz. A practical sampling rate is greater than 2B. Equality needs ideal conditions and care with signal phase at the band edge.

Worked practice

A 3 kHz message is sampled at 8 ksample/s. Find its period and check the rate.

Nyquist rate: 2 × 3 = 6 ksample/s.

Check: 8 > 6, so a spectral gap is available.

Period: 1/8000 s = 125 μs.

Why can a 100 Hz cosine look like 50 Hz when sampled at 150 samples/s?

Subtract the sample rate: 100 − 150 = −50 Hz.

Cosine is even, so the lower-frequency alias is 50 Hz.

The two cosines have identical sample values.

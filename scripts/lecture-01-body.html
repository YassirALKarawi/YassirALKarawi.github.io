1.1 What is digital communication?

Definition: Digital communication Digital communication sends information using a finite set of symbols. In a binary system, the information is represented by bits: 0 and 1.

A voice message begins as sound. A microphone changes the sound into a voltage. The system represents this voltage by numbers, sends those numbers, and uses them to recover the message. The receiver does not need an exact copy of every transmitted voltage. It must identify the correct symbols, then interpret their agreed meaning.

  1. Source. Produces information, such as voice, text or a photograph.

  2. Transmitter. Changes the information into a waveform suitable for the channel.

  3. Channel. Carries the waveform through a cable, optical fiber or free space.

  4. Receiver. Uses the received waveform to estimate the transmitted information.

  5. Destination. Uses the recovered information, for example through a loudspeaker or a screen.

Remember: Bits are labels. The channel carries a physical waveform, such as a voltage, a light signal or a radio wave.

Quick question

In a voice call, what is the source? Answer: The speaker’s message. The microphone converts the sound into an electrical signal.

1.2 From an analog signal to bits

Definition: Analog signal An analog signal represents information using a continuously varying quantity, such as microphone voltage.

Before an analog signal can be represented by bits, it passes through three different operations.

  1. Sample. Measure the voltage at regularly spaced times.

  2. Quantize. Replace each measurement by the nearest allowed level.

  3. Encode. Write the selected level as a binary word.

Together, these operations form an analog-to-digital converter (ADC). Sampling preserves the measured value at a chosen time. Quantization introduces rounding. Encoding changes the label, not the chosen level.

1.5 What do the other blocks do?

The basic path is now clear. A practical system can include extra blocks to make transmission more efficient or more reliable. In the diagram, coding prepares the data before transmission. Decoding reverses the representation after the receiver has made its decisions.

  • Source coding: represents information efficiently, for example by compressing an image.

  • Channel coding: adds structured extra bits to help detect or correct errors.

  • Pulse shaping: creates a controlled waveform from the symbols.

  • Carrier modulation: places information on a carrier when a passband channel is used.

  • Detection: decides which symbol was most likely sent.

Remember: Source coding and channel coding have different purposes. Compression reduces predictable redundancy. Error-control coding adds useful redundancy. A baseband wired link does not necessarily need radio-frequency (RF) modulation.

Simple example

A receiver observes V. The system uses V for bit 1 and V for bit 0. With a zero threshold, the receiver decides 1, because V is positive.

1.6 Communication channels

Definition: Channel A communication channel is the path through which a transmitted signal reaches a receiver.

Channel What carries the signal? Familiar example
Twisted pair Electrical signal on a pair of wires Copper network cable
Coaxial cable Electrical signal between conductor and shield Antenna feeder
Optical fiber Light in a glass fiber Optical data link
Terrestrial microwave Radio wave between ground antennas Point-to-point link
Satellite Radio wave via a satellite Satellite television
Broadcast radio Radio wave to many receivers Radio broadcasting

Remember: Choose a channel by its required distance, data rate, interference environment and installation conditions. There is no single best medium for every link.

Quick question

Is optical fiber a wireless channel because it carries light? Answer: No. The fiber guides the light, so it is a guided channel.

1.7 Guided channels: a closer look

  1. Twisted pair. Two insulated conductors are twisted together. In a balanced link, the receiver measures the voltage difference between them. Twisting helps reduce interference pickup.

  2. Coaxial cable. A central conductor is surrounded by insulation and an outer conducting shield. The geometry helps confine the electromagnetic field.

  3. Optical fiber. A core and cladding guide light. The optical signal is not affected by electromagnetic pickup in the way a copper signal is.

Compare before choosing

Copper connections carry electrical signals and need suitable protection from interference. Fiber is useful where electrical isolation or long-distance optical transmission is needed. Fiber connectors must be clean and correctly aligned.

Remember: Fiber still has losses and signal distortion. Immunity to electrical interference does not mean that every optical link is error-free.

1.8 Wireless channels and signal damage

  • Terrestrial microwave: directional antennas connect two ground sites.

  • Satellite link: a satellite relays a signal between ground stations.

  • Broadcast radio: one transmitter serves many receivers.

Any channel can change the transmitted waveform. Three effects are especially important.

Definition: Attenuation A reduction in signal amplitude or power. The received signal becomes weaker.

Definition: Distortion A change in waveform shape, for example because different frequency components experience different gains or delays.

Definition: Noise An unwanted random contribution added to the received signal.

Remember: A weak signal, a distorted signal and a noisy signal are different problems. On the next page, compare their shapes before using any equations.

1.16 Preventing aliasing and recovering a signal

Definition: Anti-aliasing filter An analog filter placed before the sampler to reduce frequency components that would fold into the wanted band.

A digital filter cannot separate two components that have already become indistinguishable through aliasing. The protection therefore starts in the analog path.

Definition: Reconstruction Reconstruction forms a continuous-time signal from its samples. Ideal reconstruction of a band-limited signal uses a low-pass interpolation filter.

The practical sequence

  1. Limit the input bandwidth with an analog filter.

  2. Sample fast enough for the filtered signal.

  3. Process or transmit the sample values.

  4. Use a reconstruction stage to form the output waveform.

Model, assumptions and reference

This teaching model uses ideal single-photon BB84 with two independently and equally likely bases. Each pulse has one detection gate. Fibre attenuation is 0.20 dB/km, receiver efficiency is 20%, and intrinsic signal error is 1.5%. The illustrative background probability is 0.0001 per gate when enabled.

T = 10−0.20 L / 10  ·  s = 0.20 T  ·  b = (1 − s) pbg

A signal registers with probability s. A gate without a signal click can register a background click with probability pbg. The model assigns at most one click per gate, with priority given to signal clicks. Background bits are random.

P(click) = s + b  ·  QBER = (0.015 s + 0.5 b) / (s + b)

The trial retains clicks with matching bases, then independently discloses each retained bit with probability 20%. Sample QBER is the number of disclosed mismatches divided by the number of disclosed bits. Finite samples fluctuate. Secret-key extraction, detector dead time, afterpulsing and connector losses are outside the model.

The Bloch sphere shows the logical qubit immediately after the selected operation. Its vertical and horizontal axes are Z and X. The arrow shows the state at each gate. Its motion between gates illustrates a transition. Drag the sphere or use its rotation controls to change the viewing angle. The circuit view assumes an ideal detected signal. The optical link samples fibre loss, receiver efficiency and background independently as specified above. The reversible CNOT path is a separate circuit example.

Circuit reference: IBM Quantum Learning, Quantum Key Distribution.

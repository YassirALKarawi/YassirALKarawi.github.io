Manuscript structure

  1. Title and authors: precise title, affiliations, corresponding author and available ORCID identifiers.
  2. Abstract and keywords: explain the problem, method, main supported findings and implications. Define abbreviations where needed.
  3. Introduction: establish the problem, relevant literature, research gap and contributions.
  4. Methods or system model: specify assumptions, symbols, algorithms, datasets, hardware and implementation details.
  5. Results and discussion: compare appropriate baselines, explain observed behavior, report uncertainty and discuss limitations.
  6. Conclusion: distinguish demonstrated findings from future work.
  7. Declarations and references: disclose funding, interests, author contributions, data and code availability.

Methods & evidence

Define metrics and units. Provide fair comparisons and justify parameter choices. Report random seeds or repeatability procedures where applicable. Identify data provenance, access conditions and relevant approvals.

Every figure and table should be cited in the text. Use readable axes, descriptive captions and accessible colour choices. Define abbreviations on first use and verify reference details.

Do not fabricate results, citations or identifiers. Describe simulation and experimental findings accurately, with clear limits to generalization.

Files to prepare

  • Editable manuscript and a matching PDF
  • Figures at publication quality and editable tables
  • A cover letter explaining fit, originality and related submissions
  • Supplementary data or code, or a justified availability statement
  • Required declarations and any permissions for reused material
